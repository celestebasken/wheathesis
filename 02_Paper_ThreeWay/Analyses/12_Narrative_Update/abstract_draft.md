# Abstract draft (150-word target)

Every number below is from `10_Rigor_Reversal/10_Reversal_Rigor.Rmd` (model estimates, 95% CI), except
the allocation means 0.24 and 0.06, which are raw cell means. Status: the three-way interaction is
the only CONFIRMATORY result; all contrasts and trend tests are EXPLORATORY.

---

Mycorrhizal outcomes range from mutualism to parasitism, a gradient usually attributed to abiotic
context. We tested whether a plant's neighbors also matter. In a greenhouse factorial (60 pots:
wheat in monoculture or mixed with fava bean, inoculated or sterile, three water levels; n = 5),
inoculation changed wheat's grain output depending on both drought and neighborhood (three-way
interaction on grain allocation, p < 0.001). Under severe drought in monoculture, inoculation
raised total productivity 38% (95% CI 11-73%) yet cut grain mass 69% (50-80%), reducing grain
allocation from 0.24 to 0.06. Under adequate water it raised grain mass in monoculture (+66%, 3-167%)
without shifting allocation. In mixed stands, no effect appeared at any water level. Colonization
and arbuscule:vesicle ratios did not explain the collapse. The sign of the AMF effect on grain
depends on the plant's neighborhood as well as its water supply.

## Notes for revision
- "Raised grain mass under adequate water" is supported (CI 3-167%) but wide; the allocation estimate
  at control (+6.1 pp, CI -1.9 to 14.2) includes zero, so the abstract does not claim an allocation
  benefit. If you want the stronger "swings from benefit to cost" wording, that is not supported for
  allocation.
- "Mixed stands: no effect at any water level" means no CI excluded zero in any currency; it is an
  absence of detectable effect at n = 5, not evidence of no effect.
- The mono-vs-mixed difference in the water trend (allocation p = 0.002, grain mass p = 0.0003) is
  exploratory and is not in the abstract; add it to Results if you want it cited.
